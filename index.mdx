# Premium real-world assets, brought on-chain

Setmos is building a new way to access high-quality real-world assets on-chain.

Set tokens bring TradFi asset categories such as treasuries, money market funds, private credit, alternative income, and gold into blockchain-based finance, with onboarding, settlement, reporting, and product controls designed for real-world use.

The goal is simple: make premium asset exposure usable by individuals, institutions, banks, fintech APIs, and on-chain investors without forcing them back into legacy financial rails every time capital needs to move.

## What Setmos makes possible

Setmos bridges the gap between premium real-world assets and programmable finance. Capital can move through on-chain settlement, while product access remains subject to onboarding, jurisdiction review, wallet policy, sanctions screening, and product-level controls.

This makes Set tokens suitable for users who want real-world yield and reserve assets inside an on-chain portfolio, and for builders who need financial products that can eventually plug into wallets, fintech applications, treasury tools, and selected DeFi venues.

## How Setmos works

Setmos is designed around a simple flow: create an account, complete onboarding, fund with USDC, mint Set tokens, monitor positions, and redeem when available under product terms.

## Beyond yield

Setmos is building Set tokens for more than passive exposure. Over time, Set tokens are designed to become usable as collateral across selected DeFi venues and protocols, subject to integration and risk review.

Future utility areas may include collateral markets, lending venues, portfolio products, cross-chain interoperability, and fintech API integrations.

## Expansion roadmap

Setmos is expanding into tokenized real estate, bitcoin yield, and gold yield, with additional premium RWA categories planned over time.

## Product disclosures and methodology

Each Set token may include product-level documentation covering asset composition, pricing methodology, custody structure, liquidity mechanics, service provider roles, fees, and risk factors.
