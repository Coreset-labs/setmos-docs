# Premium real-world assets, onchain

Setmos tokenizes high-quality real-world assets for modern portfolios.

The platform turns TradFi categories such as treasuries, money market funds, private credit, alternative income, and gold into blockchain-native products with onboarding, settlement, reporting, and product controls designed for real-world use.

Idle capital is no longer neutral. Setmos is built for users and institutions that want their cash, reserves, and long-term allocations working through transparent, regulated, and usable onchain products.

## What Setmos makes possible

Setmos bridges the gap between premium real-world assets and programmable finance. Capital can move through onchain settlement, while product availability remains subject to onboarding, jurisdiction review, wallet policy, sanctions screening, and product controls.

This makes Setmos suitable for individuals and institutions seeking real-world yield and reserve assets inside an onchain portfolio. Banks and fintech platforms may also integrate Setmos products through API-based workflows, subject to approval and product requirements.

## How Setmos works

Setmos is designed around a simple flow: create an account, complete onboarding, fund with USDC, mint assets, monitor positions, and redeem when available under product terms.

## Beyond yield

Setmos is building for more than passive exposure. Over time, its assets are designed to become usable as collateral across selected DeFi venues and protocols, subject to integration and risk review.

Future utility areas may include collateral markets, lending venues, portfolio products, cross-chain interoperability, and fintech API integrations.

## Expansion roadmap

Setmos is expanding into tokenized real estate, bitcoin yield, and gold yield, with additional premium RWA categories planned over time.

## Product disclosures and methodology

Each product may include documentation covering asset composition, pricing methodology, custody structure, liquidity mechanics, service provider roles, fees, and risk factors.
