# Premium real-world assets, brought onchain

Setmos brings high-quality real-world assets into blockchain finance.

The platform brings TradFi asset categories such as treasuries, money market funds, private credit, alternative income, and gold into blockchain finance, with onboarding, settlement, reporting, and product controls designed for real-world use.

The goal is simple: make premium asset exposure usable by individuals, institutions, banks, fintech APIs, and onchain investors without forcing them back into legacy financial rails every time capital needs to move.

## What Setmos makes possible

Setmos bridges the gap between premium real-world assets and programmable finance. Capital can move through onchain settlement, while product access remains subject to onboarding, jurisdiction review, wallet policy, sanctions screening, and product controls.

This makes Setmos suitable for users who want real-world yield and reserve assets inside an onchain portfolio, and for builders who need financial products that can eventually plug into wallets, fintech applications, treasury tools, and selected DeFi venues.

## How Setmos works

Setmos is designed around a simple flow: create an account, complete onboarding, fund with USDC, mint assets, monitor positions, and redeem when available under product terms.

## Beyond yield

Setmos is building for more than passive exposure. Over time, its assets are designed to become usable as collateral across selected DeFi venues and protocols, subject to integration and risk review.

Future utility areas may include collateral markets, lending venues, portfolio products, cross chain interoperability, and fintech API integrations.

## Expansion roadmap

Setmos is expanding into tokenized real estate, bitcoin yield, and gold yield, with additional premium RWA categories planned over time.

## Product disclosures and methodology

Each product may include documentation covering asset composition, pricing methodology, custody structure, liquidity mechanics, service provider roles, fees, and risk factors.
